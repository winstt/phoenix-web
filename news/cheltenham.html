Cheltenham Partnership Event – Key Insights

February 14, 2026  ·  Phoenix Community Trust

On 8 February 2026, Phoenix Community Trust convened a partnership event in Cheltenham bringing together representatives from over 20 community organisations, local authority partners, and independent funders from across the South West and West Midlands regions. The event, hosted at Cheltenham Town Hall, was the first in a planned series of regional convenings designed to strengthen collaboration, surface shared challenges, and explore how the Phoenix network can operate more effectively as a collective force for change.

Who attended and what was discussed

Attendees included leaders from grassroots mutual aid groups, established third-sector organisations, faith communities, and several newer organisations that have emerged from the pandemic period. Local authority colleagues from Cheltenham Borough Council and Gloucestershire County Council joined as observers and contributors — a sign of growing institutional interest in how Phoenix’s model of community-centred governance can inform statutory practice. Sessions covered three themes: accessing and navigating funding, the specific barriers facing Global Majority organisations in smaller towns and rural areas, and how peer support and mentoring can strengthen organisational resilience.

“What struck me most was how much expertise was in that room that funders rarely get to see. These organisations are not lacking solutions — they are lacking recognition and resources.” — Regional Lead, South West

Outcomes and next steps

Several concrete outcomes emerged from the day. A new peer learning cohort has been established for organisations in Gloucestershire, with monthly calls beginning in March and a shared learning resource bank being developed collaboratively. Three organisations expressed interest in forming a local consortium to apply jointly to the upcoming Phoenix grants round, and the team will provide dedicated support to help them structure that partnership. Participants also highlighted a consistent gap around communications and storytelling capacity — something Phoenix intends to address through a new resource strand launching later in the year.

The feedback from the event has been used to shape the agenda for Phoenix’s upcoming regional convenings in Bristol and Birmingham. A summary report from the Cheltenham event will be circulated to all attendees and published on our website in March. If your organisation is interested in attending a future convening or would like Phoenix to facilitate a similar event in your area, please get in touch through the Contact section of our website.
